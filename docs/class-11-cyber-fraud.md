# Class 11 · Module 4: Cyber Fraud

All eight chapters follow the approved syllabus order, each planned for a 40-minute classroom lesson. The six-scene narrated story includes a decision pause; three checked practice stages finish with an evidence record. Illustrations, captions, keyboard controls, wrong-answer feedback, retries, resume and existing teacher/student progress tracking use the established Class 11 runtime. No extra dependency or backend endpoint was added.

| Chapter | Main interaction |
| --- | --- |
| How a scam builds pressure | Map five persuasion tactics and pair concerns with independent checks |
| The internship that pays too well | Compare a synthetic offer with an independent official-source pack |
| Before you approve that payment | Inspect outgoing direction, beneficiary and amount; handle a changed payee |
| When the message looks official | Inspect actual sender and map claims to independent checks |
| A familiar voice asks for money | Branching call rehearsal, known-contact verification and calm response |
| What to do after a loss | Prompt contact plan and redacted mock incident-report builder |
| Integrated case: the festival refund | Four-module evidence map, changed evidence, three next actions and portfolio sources |
| Defend your decision | Fresh transfer case, safer revision, two-minute teacher-reviewed individual defence |

The final two chapters are the existing 80-minute annual capstone within the 32-lesson year, not additional sessions. Students link to their earlier security, AI, algorithm and payment chapters, label each source as their earlier work or a supplied replacement, and save notes/reflection. Source labels do not establish authorship: teachers inspect the actual earlier work and review the individual defence and reflection separately. Automatic marks cover structured decisions only; they are not the complete annual assessment. Written reflections are not graded by length.

All offers, .example domains, identities, amounts and transaction references are invented. There is no live QR, PIN field, payment, call, identity upload or complaint submission. The mock report is clearly labelled and never transmitted. Original evidence in a real incident should be preserved; classroom copies redact unrelated personal information. No recovery, reimbursement or fixed legal response deadline is promised. Assistance is supportive and avoids blaming victims.

## Official guidance checked 25 October 2026

- [NPCI fraud awareness](https://www.npci.org.in/fraud-awareness): receiving money does not require scanning a sender-supplied payment QR and approving a debit with a UPI PIN. The practice teaches screen direction and beneficiary rather than relying on a particular app or collect-request feature.
- [NPCI UPI FAQs](https://www.npci.org.in/what-we-do/upi/faqs): payment authorisation and UPI PIN context.
- [Navi Mumbai Police cyber-security guidance](https://navimumbaipolice.gov.in/en/citizen/cyber-security): immediate bank contact after financial fraud, preserving receipts/messages and the 1930/official portal reporting routes.
- [National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal](https://cybercrime.gov.in/): official Indian reporting destination. In a real financial cyber-fraud incident, seek prompt bank/payment-provider help and official reporting with trusted-adult support; do not delay urgent contact while preparing a perfect evidence file.
