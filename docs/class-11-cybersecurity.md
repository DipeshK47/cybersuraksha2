# Class 11 · Cybersecurity

Module 1 implements the eight chapters in the CyberSuraksha syllabus prepared on 24 October 2026. All four modules are now playable, with eight chapters each and 32 Class 11 chapters overall.

## Delivery

Each 40-minute classroom lesson uses the same story player, Rive characters, Kokoro narration, mid-story decision, three practice stages, feedback, retries and progress runtime as the earlier classes. There are no external learner accounts or real credentials in the simulations.

| Minutes | Teacher and learner activity |
| --- | --- |
| 0–8 | Play or read the six-scene story, solve its decision, retrieve prior knowledge and explain the concept. |
| 8–30 | Work through the three lab challenges. In pairs, explain a choice before testing it; discuss feedback and revise. Reserve time to challenge another pair's reasoning. |
| 30–37 | Revisit the unfamiliar third challenge independently. Use the evidence record for a brief written or spoken explanation and discuss one changed assumption. |
| 37–40 | Reflect on remaining uncertainty and a practical next action. |

The 40 minutes includes facilitated discussion and individual explanation; it is not a forced timer or a claim that every learner spends 40 minutes clicking. Fast learners should explain an alternative or changed case, rather than repeat the same answers.

## Chapter objectives and teacher checks

| Chapter | Evidence generated by the lab | Teacher follow-up / acceptable reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your digital risk map | Asset/threat/vulnerability/control chain, CIA impact map, three ranked risks with supporting evidence. | Why might the priority change? Ratings are supplied estimates, not universal measurements. A control reduces risk; it does not undo a completed disclosure. |
| Identity beyond a password | Protection plan, recovery sequence, authentication/authorisation distinctions. | Explain what remains available after a lost phone. Recovery methods must be private and independent of the lost factor. A passkey is not a password-manager brand requirement. |
| Who should have access? | Three tested access matrices, guest expiry, removal of direct and inherited former-member access. | Ask learners to justify the minimum permission from the role's actual task. A single role simulation does not replace checking every matrix row. |
| What a browser can tell you | Three hostname and request decisions with explanations. | Read the complete hostname; familiar text and HTTPS alone do not establish legitimacy. Every `.example` address is fictional. |
| Data has a lifecycle | Minimal form, restricted audiences, end-of-purpose decisions. | Explain collection purpose and who needs each field. The retention plan is an exercise policy, not a statutory period. An active complaint can justify an authorised restricted hold. |
| Devices, updates and backups | Device controls, simulated recovery from the pre-corruption copy, completeness/access checks for a fresh export. | Why can sync repeat corruption? Opening a file is insufficient: check expected records and actual access. The activity never reads, writes or deletes real learner files. |
| The suspicious login | Ordered timeline, observation/hypothesis ledger, initial-response sequence. | A city label does not identify an attacker. The sequence is a teaching scaffold; containment and escalation may overlap in a real event. Preserve relevant evidence without investigating real users. |
| Security design review | Budgeted control set, residual-risk explanation, revised response to a lost device. | Ask each learner to defend a control, its cost and remaining risk. An already active session may require revocation even when MFA protects sign-in. Accept justified alternatives in discussion. |

## Assessment and progress

The three practice stages use the existing two drill checks and one transfer check. The existing scoring policy records the first answer; retries remain visible, and learners can finish after correcting their work. Story decisions are learning activity, not substitutes for the independent transfer check.

Successful practice explanations are retained in the learner's local lesson state and shown in the evidence record on review and completion. Structured answers and feedback use the existing activity reporting path; the corresponding successful feedback is available in learner history. The local evidence record is not a new server-synchronised portfolio and does not contain a learner-authored written defence. Collect the independent spoken or written explanation through the school's normal process. Do not infer reasoning quality from button completion alone.

The eight chapters are available to Class 11 in Classroom assignment selection. Teacher previews do not create student results. No database migrations or account seeding are needed for the content change.

## Accessibility and practice boundaries

All tasks use labelled native controls or buttons with keyboard and tap support. Scene transitions and characters follow the existing reduced-motion behaviour. Captions remain available if narration is unavailable. No activity depends on colour, dragging, timing or sound alone.

Use fictional identities, payment details and logs. Do not enter passwords, recovery codes or personal records. Real incidents should go to the responsible school staff; these simulations do not contact services, make payments, submit complaints or modify accounts.

## Local verification

Use the project's Node 22.13+ runtime. Run `node --test tests/class-11-security.test.mjs` for content and evaluator checks. `scripts/class-11-security-qa.cjs` exercises the stories, incorrect feedback, practice, local resume and completion at desktop and phone widths against the local server. `tests/lms-e2e.mjs` additionally checks assignment eligibility for Class 11 against a disposable demo database.

Narration uses `scripts/story-narration.py <slug>` with the same `am_fenrir` voice, speed, loudness and real cue-time generation as the earlier chapters. Keep the Kokoro model outside the repository.
