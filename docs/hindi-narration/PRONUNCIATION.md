# Pronunciation and read-aloud notes

These are production notes. Do not append this page to a lesson recording.

Use everyday Hindi sentence rhythm. Keep a short pause after each sentence and around a displayed list. Speak English terms with familiar Indian pronunciation. Prefer the same pronunciation across all five classes.

Names follow the existing story characters. For example, Meera is मीरा, Kabir is कबीर, Tara is तारा, Neel is नील, Reva is रेवा, Nikhil is निखिल, Aditi is अदिति and Vihaan is विहान. Class 8 uses additional original characters in individual modules; retain their names as supplied.

Read square-bracketed number lists one entry at a time with small pauses. Keep the row boundaries audible where the text introduces multiple rows. Do not say “square bracket” unless that distinction is needed by the passage. Read labels such as B22 as बी बाईस, while keeping each label consistent throughout its lesson. Read an ordinary value such as 750 रुपये as सात सौ पचास रुपये.

Binary representations are different from ordinary amounts: the scripted reading of `00110010` is शून्य, शून्य, एक, एक, शून्य, शून्य, एक, शून्य. Keep the leading zeros. In the loop lesson, “count की value में 1 जोड़ें” reads the assignment naturally rather than treating the equals sign as an algebraic claim.

The script uses complete “कम से कम”, “ज़्यादा से ज़्यादा” or equivalent conditions from the source. Retain boundary words and negations. Do not simplify “गलती का पता नहीं चला” into “कोई गलती नहीं है”, or turn a small check result into a guarantee.
